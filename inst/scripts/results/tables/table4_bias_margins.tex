\footnotesize
With the margin $m_{\mathrm{avg}}$, the type I error rate averaged over a normal distribution of the bias with mean $\bar{b}$ and standard deviation $\tau$ equals $\alpha$. The margin $m_{0.95} = \bar{b} + z_{0.95}\tau$ is the 95th percentile of this distribution, so that the type I error rate given the bias is at most $\alpha$ in 95\% of trials. The critical margin is $m_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0.198$ at every $N$ (Table~\ref{tab:empirical}(a)), and margins above it are marked with $\S$; with such a margin, the SAT has less power than the RCT when the external control is unbiased. Averaged powers below the power of the RCT in the same column are marked with $\P$. Gupta et al.\ report no between-study standard deviation, so that $m_{0.95}$ and (b) are not shown for their estimates and $m_{\mathrm{avg}}$ equals $\bar{b}$; Section~\ref{S-sec:bias_uncertainty} of the Supporting Information uses the standard deviation of the trial-level differences instead. The margins are computed from $\bar{b}$ before rounding. $K$, $\ddagger$ and the abbreviations are as in Table~\ref{tab:empirical}.
